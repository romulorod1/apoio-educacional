## PT

### Explicação

#### O trabalho está na tradução

Resolver a equação é a parte mecânica. O que separa quem acerta de quem trava é a tradução: escolher
o que a incógnita representa, escrever as outras quantidades em função dela e montar a igualdade que
o enunciado descreve.

Vale sempre seguir a mesma sequência:

1. Escreva com todas as letras o que x representa, com unidade.
2. Escreva as demais quantidades usando x.
3. Monte a igualdade que o problema afirma.
4. Resolva.
5. **Volte ao enunciado** e teste cada raiz no contexto.

O passo 5 é o que este tema mais cobra. Uma equação quadrática costuma devolver duas raízes, e o
mundo real quase sempre aceita apenas uma. Medida negativa não existe, quantidade de pessoas não é
fracionária, tempo antes do lançamento não conta.

#### Um problema de números

**Exemplo 1.** O quadrado de um número menos o triplo desse número dá 40. Que número é esse?
Chamando o número de x, temos x^{2} - 3x = 40, ou seja, x^{2} - 3x - 40 = 0. As raízes são 8 e -5.
Aqui **as duas servem**, porque o enunciado fala de um número qualquer, e não de uma medida.

#### Um problema de área

**Exemplo 2.** Um retângulo tem comprimento 3 centímetros maior que a largura e área de 40
centímetros quadrados. Quais são as dimensões?
Chamando a largura de x, o comprimento é x + 3, e a área dá x · (x + 3) = 40. As raízes são 5 e -8.
A raiz -8 é **descartada**, porque largura negativa não existe. A largura é 5 e o comprimento é 8.

Repare na diferença entre os dois exemplos. A equação não sabe do que o problema fala. Quem decide
se uma raiz vale é você, lendo o enunciado de novo.

#### Um problema de contagem

**Exemplo 3.** Dois números inteiros positivos consecutivos têm produto 156. Quais são?
Chamando o menor de x, o outro é x + 1, e temos x · (x + 1) = 156. As raízes são 12 e -13, e a
negativa é descartada pela exigência de serem positivos. Os números são 12 e 13.

#### Um problema de movimento

**Exemplo 4.** A altura de uma bola, em metros, t segundos após o lançamento, é dada por
h = 20t - 5t^{2}. Em que instantes ela está a 15 metros do chão?
A equação é 20t - 5t^{2} = 15, que leva a t^{2} - 4t + 3 = 0. As raízes são 1 e 3, e as duas fazem
sentido: a bola passa por essa altura na subida e de novo na descida.

#### Erros comuns

**Responder com a incógnita e não com o que foi perguntado.** Se x é a largura e o problema pede a
área, ainda falta uma conta.

**Aceitar as duas raízes por automatismo.** Cada raiz precisa ser testada contra o contexto, e a
justificativa do descarte faz parte da resposta.

**Descartar a raiz negativa por automatismo.** Em problema de número puro ou de temperatura, o valor
negativo pode ser a resposta certa.

**Perder a unidade no caminho.** Se as medidas estão em metros, a área sai em metros quadrados.

## EN

### Explanation

#### The work is in the translation

Solving the equation is the mechanical part. What separates the students who get there from the ones
who freeze is the translation: choosing what the unknown stands for, writing the other quantities in
terms of it and building the equality the problem describes.

It is always worth following the same sequence:

1. Write out in full what x stands for, with its unit.
2. Write the other quantities using x.
3. Build the equality the problem states.
4. Solve it.
5. **Go back to the problem** and test each root against the context.

Step 5 is what this topic asks for most. A quadratic equation usually returns two roots, and the
real world almost always accepts only one. A negative measurement does not exist, a number of people
is not fractional, time before the launch does not count.

#### A problem about numbers

**Example 1.** The square of a number minus three times that number gives 40. What is the number?
Calling the number x, we get x^{2} - 3x = 40, that is, x^{2} - 3x - 40 = 0. The roots are 8 and -5.
Here **both work**, because the problem talks about any number, not about a measurement.

#### A problem about area

**Example 2.** A rectangle is 3 centimetres longer than it is wide and has an area of 40 square
centimetres. What are its dimensions?
Calling the width x, the length is x + 3, and the area gives x · (x + 3) = 40. The roots are 5 and
-8. The root -8 is **discarded**, because a negative width does not exist. The width is 5 and the
length is 8.

Notice the difference between the two examples. The equation knows nothing about what the problem is
talking about. You are the one who decides whether a root counts, by reading the problem again.

#### A problem about counting

**Example 3.** Two consecutive positive integers have product 156. What are they?
Calling the smaller one x, the other is x + 1, and we get x · (x + 1) = 156. The roots are 12 and
-13, and the negative one is discarded by the requirement that they be positive. The numbers are 12
and 13.

#### A problem about motion

**Example 4.** The height of a ball, in metres, t seconds after being thrown, is given by
h = 20t - 5t^{2}. At what times is it 15 metres above the ground?
The equation is 20t - 5t^{2} = 15, which leads to t^{2} - 4t + 3 = 0. The roots are 1 and 3, and
both make sense: the ball passes that height on the way up and again on the way down.

#### Common mistakes

**Answering with the unknown instead of what was asked.** If x is the width and the problem asks for
the area, there is still one calculation left.

**Accepting both roots automatically.** Every root has to be tested against the context, and
justifying the one you discard is part of the answer.

**Discarding the negative root automatically.** In a pure number problem or a temperature problem,
the negative value may well be the right answer.

**Losing the unit along the way.** If the measurements are in metres, the area comes out in square
metres.

### Exercises

**Block A. Fundamentals**

1. The square of a number minus three times that number gives 40. Find the possible values.
2. Two consecutive positive integers have product 156. Find both numbers.
3. A rectangle is 3 centimetres longer than it is wide and has an area of 40 square centimetres.
   Find its dimensions.
4. The sum of a number and its square is 42. Find the possible values.
5. A square has an area of 121 square centimetres. Find the length of its side.

**Block B. Building up**

6. A triangle has base x centimetres and height 4 centimetres less than the base, and its area is 30
   square centimetres. Find the base and the height.
7. A rectangular plot has a perimeter of 60 metres and an area of 200 square metres. Find its
   dimensions.
8. Marina's age 5 years from now, multiplied by the age she was 5 years ago, gives 96. Find her age
   today.
9. The height of a ball, in metres, t seconds after being thrown, is given by h = 20t - 5t^{2}.
   Find the times at which it is 15 metres above the ground.
10. The height of an object, in metres, t seconds after being launched, is given by h = 30t - 5t^{2}.
    Find the time at which it returns to the ground.
11. A square garden is surrounded by a path 2 metres wide all the way round. The region made of the
    garden and the path is a square of 144 square metres. Find the side of the garden.
12. A group of friends split a bill of 240 reais equally. If there were 2 more friends in the group,
    each would pay 10 reais less. Find how many friends there are.
13. A rectangle has a diagonal of 25 centimetres and a perimeter of 70 centimetres. Find its sides.
14. The sum of a positive number and its reciprocal is the fraction 26/5. Find that number.

**Block C. Going further**

15. From a rectangular sheet 20 by 12 centimetres a border of constant width x is cut off all the
    way round, leaving a rectangle of 84 square centimetres. Find x and justify discarding the other
    root.
16. The sum of the first n positive integers is given by S = n · (n + 1)/2. Find how many terms are
    needed for that sum to be 210.
17. A right triangle has legs measuring x and x + 2 centimetres, and hypotenuse x + 4 centimetres.
    Find the three sides and justify discarding the other root.
18. Two numbers have sum 12 and product 27. Write the quadratic equation having those two numbers as
    roots and find the numbers.
19. A rectangle has a perimeter of 40 metres. Show that the largest possible area is 100 square
    metres and explain why no rectangle of perimeter 40 metres has an area of 120 square metres.
20. A rectangular pool 10 by 6 metres is surrounded by a path of constant width x all the way round.
    The area of the path is 36 square metres. Find the width of the path.

### Answer key

1. 8 or -5. Both work, because the problem talks about a number and not about a measurement.
2. 12 and 13. The root -13 is discarded by the requirement that the numbers be positive.
3. Width 5 centimetres and length 8 centimetres. The root -8 is discarded because a negative width
   does not exist.
4. 6 or -7. Both values work, since this is about any number.
5. 11 centimetres. The root -11 is discarded because a negative side does not exist.
6. Base 10 centimetres and height 6 centimetres. The root -6 is discarded because a negative base
   does not exist.
7. 10 metres by 20 metres. With perimeter 60, the sum of two neighbouring sides is 30.
8. 11 years old. The root -11 is discarded because a negative age does not exist. Indeed,
   16 × 6 = 96.
9. 1 second and 3 seconds. Both times count: the ball passes that height on the way up and on the
   way down.
10. 6 seconds. The root zero corresponds to the moment of the launch, not to the return to the
    ground.
11. 8 metres. The larger square has side 12, which is the side of the garden plus 2 metres on each
    side, and the root -16 is discarded because a negative side does not exist.
12. 6 friends. The root -8 is discarded because a negative number of people does not exist.
    With 6 friends each pays 40 reais, and with 8 friends each would pay 30 reais, a difference of
    10 reais.
13. 15 centimetres and 20 centimetres. The perimeter gives sum 35, and the diagonal gives sum of
    squares 625, which yields product 300.
14. 5. The other root is the fraction 1/5, which is also positive, but the problem asks for a
    number, and the two values form the same pair of number and reciprocal.
15. x = 3 centimetres. The other root is 13, discarded because a border 13 centimetres wide is
    wider than the sheet of 12 centimetres itself.
16. 20 terms. The root -21 is discarded because the number of terms is a positive number.
17. The sides are 6, 8 and 10 centimetres. The root -2 is discarded because a negative leg does not
    exist.
18. The equation is x^{2} - 12x + 27 = 0, and the numbers are 3 and 9.
19. The largest area is 100 square metres, reached by the square of side 10 metres. For area 120 the
    equation becomes x^{2} - 20x + 120 = 0, whose discriminant is -80. Since the discriminant is
    negative, no such rectangle exists.
20. 1 metre. The root -9 is discarded because a negative width does not exist. Indeed, the
    outer rectangle becomes 12 by 8, and 96 - 60 = 36.
